# Act I — The Hidden Shore

**Game:** *Bells of the Black Chrysanthemum*<br>
**Status:** proposed act-level restructuring draft<br>
**Supersedes on approval:** the current prologue opening in `03-beats-outline.md` and the placement of Nikola's arrival in the current Takamine chapter<br>
**Target play time:** 35–50 minutes on a first playthrough<br>
**Field format:** top-down exploration, stealth, investigation, and dialogue<br>
**Combat format:** side-view real-time action with free movement, authored animation commitment, and post-animation attack cooldowns
**Metroidvania boundary:** no movement ability gates, backtracking requirement, or map-completion progression

## Act promise

Nikola Dražanić enters southern Japan on a blacked-out boat, expecting to begin a righteous hunt. The first people he meets do not need him to explain persecution to them, and they do not consent to becoming his army. He earns a place on the road only after placing his violence under local limits.

The act begins as covert Gothic infiltration, becomes a tense argument over who bears the cost of resistance, and ends with Nikola and Ren fighting together for the first time.

## Dramatic question

Can Nikola offer protection without treating other people's danger as proof of his authority?

## Act functions

1. Introduce Nikola as the initial player character and establish the Severed Dragon Lance.
2. Teach the top-down field grammar before shifting into side-view combat.
3. Establish the hidden Christian communities as organized people with their own leaders, disagreements, routes, and refusal rights.
4. Introduce Ren as a fast, practical local operator who distrusts heroic abstractions.
5. Establish Kurozane's black-bell apparatus through patrol behavior rather than exposition.
6. Form the first active combat pair: Nikola and Ren.
7. Point the party toward a court-licensed Christian settlement whose protection has begun to fail.

## Entry state

- Alternate Genna-era Japan is under Shogun Kurozane's black-bell security regime.
- Christian households survive through concealed practice, negotiated compromises, mutual aid, and deliberate silence.
- Nikola has followed a damaged Dražanić ledger through Ragusa, Lisbon, Goa, Melaka, and Macao.
- He speaks Croatian, Italian, Latin, and rough Portuguese, but almost no Japanese.
- He knows an immortal ruler has reproduced part of the Dracul blood-sovereignty pattern in Japan.
- He does not yet understand the bell system, local authority, or the human cost of announcing a crusade.
- Ren knows the roads, patrol habits, and household warning codes. He has no reason to trust Nikola.

## Non-negotiable narrative rules

- Nikola is brave, sincere, disciplined in his personal life, and morally serious. His flaw is not secret villainy; it is believing his willingness to die gives him the right to volunteer others.
- The hidden village is never treated as passive scenery waiting for a European savior.
- The village head may refuse Nikola, revise his plan, withhold information, or end a conversation without creating a failure state.
- Ren does not join because Nikola converts him. He joins because the court threatens people he knows and Nikola accepts enforceable limits.
- A detected stealth route changes pressure and resources; it does not cause an arbitrary restart.
- No required civilian dies to motivate the heroes.
- Local Christian practice is not framed as corrupted merely because it is Japanese or adapted to concealment.
- Violence is quick, readable, and dangerous. The field is not a full side-scrolling Metroidvania.

## Principal cast

### Lord Nikola Dražanić

Nikola arrives with an inflated title, an authentic martial education, a suspect family legend, and a weapon designed to check supernatural sovereignty. He can kill monsters. He cannot read the village's warning markers, understand a refusal spoken in Japanese, or distinguish a court informant from a frightened fisher without help.

His Act I movement is from **self-appointed commander** to **dangerous specialist under conditions**.

### Ren Ishikawa

Ren is a courier, climber, knife fighter, and opportunistic route reader. His side-view movement should evoke the improvisational mobility of a thief without copying an existing character: rapid wall rebounds inside authored arenas, low vaults, rope-assisted direction changes, and short-range Cut/Pierce attacks.

His Act I movement is from **keep the danger moving past us** to **intercept it before it chooses the next household**.

### The village head

The role is canonical; the personal name remains open until the wider southern-region cast is planned. The head is a lay community leader rather than a priest and speaks for the village only within an acknowledged local process. Other households can disagree.

The head's position is rational: open militancy may give the court the public enemy it needs while exposing children, elders, routes, and food stores.

### The boat captain

A coastal smuggler who has completed a bounded contract, not Nikola's loyal retainer. The captain will not wait offshore for a heroic escape. This establishes that every route has an owner and a limit.

## Location identity

### The black approach

A moonless coast of basalt teeth, reed channels, low fishing lights, and one distant bell glow. Nikola's boat has its bright fittings wrapped in cloth. The Lance travels inside a weathered mast-and-reliquary case.

### The hidden village

The settlement must first read as a functioning fishing and salt village:

- drying racks, boat repair, smokehouses, a well, family storehouses, and a storm shrine;
- concealed Christian signs that are meaningful only in relationship, not as collectible exotica;
- no obvious church silhouette;
- a prayer room that can return to ordinary household use within minutes;
- more than one exit route, each controlled by different households;
- children, elders, and working adults present as people with tasks rather than generic victims.

The village is fictional. It should evoke a southern coastal environment without borrowing the name or exact suffering of a historical persecuted community.

## Scene specifications

### `a1-01-black-water` — Black Water

**Opening image:** Nikola kneels beside the wrapped Lance as the boat captain cuts the last visible lantern.

The captain gives three pieces of information and refuses a fourth:

- bells now answer one another along the coast;
- soldiers inspect foreign cargo more carefully than foreign graves;
- one village still honors an old landing code;
- the captain will not identify the household that purchased the route.

This is the movement tutorial. Nikola crosses the moving deck, secures loose cargo, examines the disguised Lance case, and chooses which personal item to keep accessible:

- ward chalk: one extra anti-Umbral field use;
- pistol powder: one ranged interruption in the first battle;
- Latin ledger leaf: additional dialogue evidence, but no combat benefit.

No choice is permanently optimal. Each makes a later problem more legible.

**Dialogue anchor**

> **Captain:** I was paid to bring one foreigner to one dark shore. I was not paid to believe his reasons.
>
> **Nikola:** Sensible. Belief is expensive.
>
> **Captain:** Then do not spend mine.

### `a1-02-three-landings` — Three Landings

The player selects one of three short routes:

| Route | Movement test | Immediate benefit | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reed channel | slow water, sight-line breaks | keeps foreign silhouette concealed | wet powder if it was selected |
| Basalt shelf | exact footing and timed wave gaps | preserves equipment and finds a patrol token | a fall creates noise and raises patrol alert |
| Customs path | disguise, observation, and one social bluff through the captain | reveals official bell terminology | Nikola's face is described in a coastal report |

Detection produces a compact pursuit and changes `patrol_alert`; it does not reload the scene. Nikola can hide the Lance, draw attention away from the landing code, or confront one scout. Killing a human scout is not required and creates a later question about the missing patrol.

### `a1-03-village-without-a-cross` — The Village Without a Cross

Nikola recognizes Christian material where he expects Christian declarations, then repeatedly misreads how the community uses it. The player must observe before acting:

- a fish arrangement marks a safe door only after a household confirms the tide;
- a Latin fragment is a mnemonic held by a reader, not proof that Nikola owns the conversation;
- a hidden icon can be exposed by an impatient interaction;
- an apparently empty storehouse is a divided archive whose custodians do not know every route.

If Nikola exposes a sign, the village calmly removes it and moves the meeting. The consequence is reduced trust and extra labor, not a melodramatic execution.

The village head verifies Nikola through the landing token, the Dražanić ledger leaf if carried, and the fact that he does not know the correct local response. His ignorance is evidence that he is foreign, not evidence that he is harmless.

### `a1-04-seven-roads-ren` — Ren of Seven Roads

Ren first appears above Nikola's route, having watched him pass two decoy signs. He takes the landing token and forces Nikola into a short pursuit through drying racks and roof edges. This is not a boss battle.

The encounter teaches Ren's movement before he is playable:

- low obstacle vault;
- short wall rebound;
- rope turn around an overhead anchor;
- downward knife feint that cuts a hanging alarm cord rather than a person.

Ren stops when Nikola chooses to protect the concealed door instead of continuing the chase. If Nikola prioritizes the token, Ren still ends the chase but enters the council with less trust.

Translation is handled through a village reader with rough Portuguese. Ren and Nikola cannot have effortless banter yet. Their early comedy comes from gesture, mutually obvious insults, and the reader declining to translate rank.

**Dialogue anchor**

> **Nikola:** Tell him I am Count Dražanić.
>
> **Reader:** I told him you have a long name and a weapon you were asked not to uncover.
>
> **Ren:** Better.

### `a1-05-no-one-volunteered` — No One Volunteered

Nikola lays out his warning:

- Kurozane's bell network is vampiric, not merely administrative;
- court patrols are searching for a foreign ledger and its counter-rite;
- communities that wait for the completed network will be catalogued, isolated, and consumed;
- passive concealment alone cannot end the threat.

The village head answers:

- concealment has kept named people alive;
- Nikola does not know which households can move, who is ill, or where winter food is stored;
- his family weapon is not a local chain of command;
- resistance may be necessary, but necessity does not appoint him.

The player chooses Nikola's posture, not the village's answer:

1. **Ask for a bounded task.** Raises `chief_consent`; Nikola receives the patrol schedule.
2. **Invoke the hunter covenant.** Reveals more Dražanić lore; the head demands the Lance remain wrapped inside the village.
3. **Challenge the cost of waiting.** Ren partly agrees, but the village withholds a route until Nikola proves he can obey a stop signal.

All three proceed. The choice establishes tone and alters who provides information during the alarm.

### `a1-06-bell-search` — The Bell Search

A court search team arrives early because the coastal relay detected either Nikola, the boat, or a missing patrol token. The team includes human officers, one Ash Oni tracker, and a portable black bell that reacts to concealed names.

The village head issues a stop condition: the fight must leave the residential lane and must not expose the prayer room. Nikola may propose but does not choose alone.

The player selects the interception plan:

| Plan | Top-down setup | Side-view arena | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Draw them to the salt cut | lay a false trail and move the Lance openly outside the village | narrow salt terraces with one vertical rope route for Ren | best village secrecy; highest combat pressure |
| Break the bell at the boat yard | evacuate workers and prepare cover | broad ground plane with hanging hull obstacles | easiest bell access; damage to village livelihood |
| Hold the storm gate | reinforce the lane and place household escape signals | defensive threshold with civilians moving behind opaque cover | lowest patrol pursuit; exposes that the village is organized |

**Combat teaching**

- Nikola moves freely when his attack animation has ended, even while his attack cooldown is still counting down.
- His thrust is precise and committed; mashing cannot skip recovery.
- Ren joins as a temporary tag partner after the first enemy wave.
- Ren's mobility creates angles while Nikola controls a lane.
- The player must break the portable bell object, not merely empty every health bar.
- The Ash Oni's Ember hide teaches delivery and essence feedback.
- Human officers can flee or surrender; killing them is never required for victory.

**Failure handling**

- If the bell completes one pulse, an exposed household route must relocate after battle.
- If the boat-yard structures take heavy damage, the village loses supplies but no required character dies.
- If Nikola exposes the Lance, Kurozane's apparatus gains a positive identification.
- Defeat restarts at the side-view encounter cut, not at the beginning of the act.

### `a1-07-compact-at-dawn` — Compact at Dawn

The search team has been stopped, but the village is not transformed into a militant base. The village head states the compact:

1. Nikola may follow the court route away from the village.
2. He will not publish the village's location or treat its households as recruits.
3. Ren may travel with him by Ren's choice.
4. Ren carries an independent stop signal and can abandon a plan that redirects unacceptable danger.
5. Any evidence taken from the village is a route-safe copy; the original remains under divided local custody.

Ren joins because the portable bell carried a destination mark for **Quiet Cross**, a settlement whose licensed Christian practice was supposed to protect it. If the court is searching it anyway, hiding and compliance are both being converted into fuel.

**Closing image:** the boat departs without Nikola. Ren cuts the foreign wrapping from only the Lance's grip, leaving its head covered until they have cleared the village boundary.

**Dialogue anchor**

> **Ren:** You wanted soldiers.
>
> **Nikola:** I found one.
>
> **Ren:** No. You found a road. I decide whether I am still on it.

## Act I combat contract

### Active roster

- Player begins with Nikola only.
- Ren becomes a temporary tag partner during `a1-06`.
- Ren becomes a permanent active partner after `a1-07`.
- Aya's later passive-healer function and Miyo's later role are not introduced here.

### Nikola's teaching kit

| Verb | Function | Commitment | Cooldown lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hunter Thrust | direct Pierce attack | short wind-up, one active frame group, readable recovery | basic attacks still have a nonzero cooldown |
| Severing Arc | upward Radiance/Pierce check | commits Nikola in place | anti-undead power is punishable if used without spacing |
| Ward Step | short guarded advance | movement/defense, not an attack cancel | remains useful while weapon cooldown runs |
| Lance Reveal | encounter-specific bell break | long commitment | power solves the object but raises `lance_exposed` |

### Ren's teaching kit

| Verb | Function | Commitment | Cooldown lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Road Knife | quick Cut sequence | short range and modest lock | fast does not mean cooldown-free |
| Hook Turn | rope-assisted reposition | requires an authored anchor | movement technique, not universal flight |
| Low Vault | crosses a low body or obstacle | no damage | arena traversal without Metroidvania gating |
| Back Mark | marks enemy facing for Nikola | positional setup | rewards tag timing over simultaneous button spam |

## Choice and consequence matrix

| Decision | Immediate reaction | Act II carry-forward | Cannot do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing route | changes evidence, resources, and alert | modifies opening patrol density | cannot expose the village by itself |
| Council posture | changes trust and information source | changes who vouches for Nikola | cannot force village enlistment |
| Interception plan | changes arena, property loss, and secrecy | changes refugee/resource conditions | cannot require civilian death |
| Lance use | makes bell break easier | Kurozane's agents may identify the hunter line | cannot permanently lose the Lance |
| Treatment of human patrol | surrender, escape, or death recorded | changes later testimony and pursuit | cannot block story progression |
| Carried evidence | choose schedule, token, or copied name fragment | changes optional dialogue/evidence in Act II | cannot remove the Quiet Cross lead |

## Narrative handoff candidates

These are compact act-level candidates, not a final runtime schema:

- `village_secrecy`: `intact | strained | exposed-organization`
- `chief_consent`: `bounded | wary`
- `ren_trust`: bounded integer `0–2`
- `lance_exposed`: boolean
- `patrol_alert`: bounded integer `0–2`
- `families_relocated`: boolean
- `human_patrol_outcome`: `withdrew | surrendered | killed-mixed`
- `carried_evidence`: `schedule | token | name-fragment`

No later ending should depend on one hidden “good choice.” These values should alter witnesses, costs, available proof, and dialogue support.

## Required art, animation, level, and audio

### Field environments

- black-water boat deck;
- three coastal landing variants assembled from one tileset;
- working fishing village day/night states;
- council interior;
- three alarm-route overlays.

### Side-view battle stage

One modular coastal stage with three authored configurations:

- salt terraces;
- boat yard;
- storm gate.

Each version needs a stable ground plane, limited authored platforms, clear collision edges, a destructible bell relay, and no inaccessible visual ledges that imply Metroidvania traversal.

### Character animation minimum

- Nikola: field walk, careful step, inspect, concealed-carry, Lance draw; combat idle, run, jump, guard step, thrust, Severing Arc, hit, fall.
- Ren: field roof run, crouch, rope turn, alarm cut; combat idle, run, jump, low vault, Road Knife, Hook Turn, Back Mark, hit, tag entry.
- Village head and reader: at least two non-identical conversation poses.
- Ash Oni tracker: sniff/track telegraph, pounce, Ember hide flare, stagger.

### Audio motifs

- wrapped metal and hull creak for Nikola's approach;
- village work rhythm with the Christian melodic fragment hidden inside an ordinary labor pattern;
- first black-bell pulse should interrupt, not replace, the village soundscape;
- Ren's movement uses rope, sandal, and knife detail rather than magical whooshes.

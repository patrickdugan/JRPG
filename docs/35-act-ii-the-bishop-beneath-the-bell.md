# Act II — The Bishop Beneath the Bell

**Game:** *Bells of the Black Chrysanthemum*<br>
**Status:** proposed act-level restructuring draft<br>
**Depends on:** `34-act-i-the-hidden-shore.md`<br>
**Supersedes on approval:** the current placement and framing of the first Father Mateus boss at Takamine<br>
**Target play time:** 55–75 minutes on a first playthrough<br>
**Field format:** top-down village defense, social investigation, church/crypt descent<br>
**Combat format:** side-view real-time action with Nikola/Ren tag play and a nonlethal Mateus boss
**Metroidvania boundary:** authored routes and optional side rooms, but no ability-gated campaign progression

## Naming lock

The canonical working name remains **Father Mateus Avelar**. “Matthias” in brainstorming refers to the same character. If a more formal church form is wanted later, **Matthaeus Avelar** can appear in a Latin record without renaming the character in dialogue.

## Act promise

Nikola and Ren enter a Christian settlement under attack expecting to defend believers from an outside persecutor. Beneath its church they discover a harder structure: Mateus has preserved a licensed form of Christian life by rewriting its public administration around Kurozane's surveillance.

He is the last widely connected European priest in the region, a de facto bishop without formal episcopal authority, the official interpreter of a tolerated foreign doctrine, and the inquisitor sent when protected congregations stop obeying. He has saved lives. He has also made their survival legible to a vampire state.

The act culminates in a fast, Gothic duel beneath the bell. Nikola bests Mateus without killing him. Mateus recognizes the Severed Dragon covenant and realizes the hunter who crossed the world is not merely another foreign soldier.

## Dramatic question

When survival has been purchased through deception and surveillance, who has the right to decide whether the bargain continues?

## Act functions

1. Show why Mateus's compromise was persuasive without treating it as harmless.
2. Establish the difference between adapted local faith and coercive state corruption.
3. Introduce the court's Christian licensing system as one branch of its broader blood bureaucracy.
4. Make Mateus a frightening boss, a capable priest, a compromised administrator, and a personally cowardly man at the same time.
5. Let Nikola's sincere faith and personal discipline give him real moral force without making him automatically correct about strategy.
6. Use Ren as the person who notices what both Europeans omit: names, routes, cells, and who gets to decide after the duel.
7. Reveal the Dražanić–Dracul–Severed Dragon echo through recognition rather than a lore lecture.
8. Begin the Nikola–Mateus abrasive partnership through a costly action, not instant forgiveness.
9. Establish Mateus's live family stake without turning his descendants into excuses for him.

## Entry state

- Nikola and Ren travel under the hidden village's compact.
- They carry one route-safe piece of evidence pointing to Quiet Cross.
- Nikola's Lance may or may not have been positively identified.
- Quiet Cross is a licensed Christian settlement: its rites are permitted so long as its households remain registered and politically obedient.
- One congregation removed a court obedience clause from its copied catechism and sheltered unregistered relatives.
- Kurozane's officials have declared the settlement in breach.
- Mateus has been sent to restore compliance before an Oni purification force destroys the village.
- The force has arrived early, either by policy or because Kurozane intends to prove that Mateus's guarantees mean nothing.

## Non-negotiable narrative rules

- “Heretic village” is a hostile in-world label used by officials and factional enemies, not the production document's neutral description.
- Japanese Christian practice is not corrupt because it is Japanese, hidden, oral, or adapted to persecution.
- The corruption is coercive and administrative: registers become targeting tools, confession becomes intelligence, and protection is conditioned on obedience to Kurozane.
- Mateus does not secretly control every Christian in Japan. He has a far-reaching network, scarce sacramental authority, foreign literacy, and court recognition that allow him to behave like a bishop.
- Mateus has genuinely prevented deaths. The same system has enabled disappearances.
- The villagers are not required to forgive, recruit, or surrender Mateus because the heroes win a duel.
- Nikola's moral clarity matters. His victory does not transfer custody of the church or its people to him.
- Mateus is defeated nonlethally in this act.
- Ren and the village council remain present in the post-boss judgment.
- Live family creates restraint, fear, and responsibility. It does not automatically redeem anyone.

## The licensed faith

### What Quiet Cross is allowed to keep

- baptism, marriage, burial, household prayer, and an altered public Mass;
- local Japanese names, songs, feast customs, and oral teaching;
- concealed devotional objects so long as their custodians are registered;
- a recognized lay council;
- Mateus's periodic sacramental visits.

### What Kurozane's system inserts

- a black-chrysanthemum approval seal in the household register;
- a declaration that the shogun's order is providential protection against chaos;
- confession summaries stripped of sacramental detail but still used to flag dissent and movement;
- a bell mark for every household receiving protection;
- a threshold ward made with court blood that repels lesser Oni while making the building visible to the bell network;
- collective punishment when a registered household shelters an unregistered person.

The threshold blood ward is separate from the Eucharist. The story should not imply that Japanese Christians unknowingly perform a vampiric sacrament. Mateus's sin is already severe: he concealed the ward's surveillance cost and represented a revocable state license as pastoral safety.

### Why people accepted it

- unlicensed settlements were being burned;
- the ward did repel uncontrolled ghouls and lesser Oni;
- registered marriages and burials reduced arbitrary seizures for a time;
- Mateus used court access to ransom prisoners and divert raids;
- families made different choices under pressure, and some still judge the bargain worth its cost.

### Why it is failing

- the court demands more names each season;
- missing quotas are filled with accusation;
- the bell network no longer distinguishes protection from inventory;
- Kurozane benefits when Mateus cannot keep his promises;
- Quiet Cross has begun hiding people from its own register.

## Principal cast

### Father Mateus Avelar

Mateus is Portuguese-born, Jesuit-trained, and physically forty-three in appearance. He remained after the expulsion order, survived clandestinely, accepted Kurozane's blood, and converted his language, routes, and priestly trust into administrative power.

His self-description is **the man who kept a remnant alive**. The act's evidence supports that claim and exposes what he leaves out.

His Act II movement is from **sole interpreter of necessary compromise** to **defeated witness whose access is useful only under other people's authority**.

### Nikola Dražanić

Nikola encounters a European Christian whose education, language, and fear he understands. This removes Mateus's easiest defense: that outsiders cannot comprehend the compromise. Nikola can call the corrupted administration what it is while still having to obey the settlement's judgment after the fight.

His Act II movement is from **I have found the traitor** to **victory creates a duty to expose and constrain, not a private right to execute**.

### Ren Ishikawa

Ren has little patience for a theological contest that forgets the people locked below it. He maps the crypt, opens routes, tracks which records are copied, and interrupts both Europeans whenever they turn the village into an argument about themselves.

His Act II movement is from **Nikola needs supervision** to **these two dangerous men may be useful if neither is allowed to own the mission**.

### Quiet Cross council

The council contains licensed believers, concealed believers, one household that wants the old bargain restored, and one that wants Mateus exposed. The council is not morally homogenized.

Its members decide:

- which records may leave;
- who guards Mateus after the duel;
- whether the public church remains open;
- which families relocate;
- whether Nikola and Ren receive the next route.

### Miyo Senda

Miyo is Mateus's literal granddaughter through his half-Japanese daughter and the Japanese Senda household. She is mostly Japanese, publicly and personally **Miyo Senda**, and is not proof that Mateus deserves forgiveness.

Act II should establish her existence without forcing her into the active party. The recommended treatment is an indirect but unmistakable family clue:

- a Senda weather table in the crypt corrects Mateus's older navigation hand;
- an execution record names Mateus's rebellious son;
- a separate protected note shows that his daughter survived into the Senda household;
- Mateus becomes visibly more afraid when Nikola notices the second record than when the Lance is at his throat.

An on-screen Miyo cameo remains an open full-act decision. Her competence must precede any ancestry reveal if she appears.

## Location identity

### Quiet Cross

The village is visibly Christian but marked by state permission:

- a modest church assembled from local materials;
- black-chrysanthemum license plaques beside household tallies;
- a public bell that is too new for the building;
- a lane of registered homes and a less visible ring of sheltered relatives;
- an infirmary, granary, graveyard, and council room with practical defensive value;
- signs of an interrupted ordinary day rather than a tableau of anonymous suffering.

### Church above

The sanctuary remains a sincere place used by sincere believers. Altered text appears in marginal insertions, official response cards, and the license display. The architecture should not imply that the building itself is evil.

### Crypt below

The lower complex reveals Mateus's double office:

- protected graves and concealed relics;
- food, medicine, and letters he genuinely preserved;
- interview cells and restraint points;
- confiscated rosaries tagged by household;
- duplicate registers cross-indexed to bell marks;
- a blood-fed threshold machine beneath the public bell;
- a private altar stripped down to the items Mateus would not let Kurozane touch.

The contradiction is spatial: refuge, archive, prison, and control room occupy the same foundation.

## Scene specifications

### `a2-01-smoke-over-quiet-cross` — Smoke Over Quiet Cross

The party sees a legal Christian emblem and smoke in the same frame. A wounded runner uses the hidden village's route code but adds a black seal meaning **licensed protection**.

The player may enter by:

- the grave lane, reaching trapped elders first;
- the drainage cut, reaching the bell anchor first;
- the public gate, drawing attackers toward Nikola.

The choice determines the opening position and which witness speaks first. It does not decide who lives through a single opaque prompt.

The attack includes human auxiliaries and Ash Oni. Their writ says Quiet Cross is in **doctrinal breach**, the official source of the “heretic” label.

### `a2-02-defend-the-living` — Defend the Living

The battle objective has three simultaneous pressures:

1. prevent an Oni anchor from locking the escape lane;
2. open two civilian exits;
3. preserve one vulnerable evidence site after the people are moving.

The player chooses which evidence site survives intact:

| Site | Evidence gained | Cost of losing it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| public bell plaque | proves the state promised protection | later argument relies on testimony instead |
| household register | proves missing and inserted names | council destroys or divides the damaged book |
| ward threshold | proves the supernatural tracking mechanism | Mateus can initially deny how the court found the village |

People always outrank evidence in objective evaluation. The game must never reward leaving a trapped person to save a book.

### `a2-03-approved-mass` — The Approved Mass

The surviving council shelters people inside the church. A licensed response card is still set beside a sincere household prayer. The player investigates four authorities:

- the printed public clause;
- the oral version remembered by an elder;
- the court register;
- the threshold ward.

Nikola recognizes which Latin phrases Mateus translated and which were added by court clerks. Ren discovers that household movements, not beliefs, are the register's most valuable data.

The council does not ask Nikola whether the faith is real. It asks whether the ward can be severed without drawing every marked Oni to the church. His answer is incomplete: the Lance can break the bond, but he needs its originating seal below.

The council grants descent under three terms:

1. open cells before seizing records;
2. do not destroy the register until the council decides whether it is evidence or a weapon;
3. bring Mateus back alive if that remains possible.

### `a2-04-under-the-bell` — Under the Bell

The crypt is an authored top-down dungeon with optional evidence rooms, not a traversal-power maze.

Core route:

- protected graves;
- supply vault;
- two interview cells;
- register crossing;
- blood-threshold machine;
- interview chapel.

Optional route discoveries:

- proof Mateus diverted one purge and failed to divert another;
- confiscated letters he never forwarded;
- the Senda weather table;
- the son's execution record;
- a ledger showing that Kurozane has raised the required household quota three times.

Ren can reach short flanking paths with rope anchors, but Nikola can open the critical path by ordinary switches and ward interactions. Ren's mobility yields evidence and combat position, not mandatory Metroidvania ability gating.

### `a2-05-the-inquisitor` — The Inquisitor

Mateus is found questioning three believers who removed Kurozane's obedience clause. He has not tortured them for spectacle. He has isolated them, threatened excommunication, and demanded the names of sheltered relatives because he believes a completed list will let him negotiate the raid down to arrests.

The detainees state that the list will become a death map.

Mateus's defense has four layers:

1. **Historical:** open profession brought massacres.
2. **Pastoral:** his rites, ransoms, and wards kept families alive.
3. **Consequential:** an incomplete list will cause collective punishment.
4. **Personal:** he has already lost a son and believes defiant courage is often paid for by somebody else's child.

Nikola's answer is strongest when it stays concrete:

- protection that can be revoked at a quota is ownership;
- confession cannot be turned into intelligence and still be called trust;
- Kurozane manufactures the danger Mateus cites;
- fear for living family does not authorize trading other families' names.

Ren cuts through the climax by opening the last cell and asking the detainees whether they consent to Mateus's bargain. They do not agree among themselves. That disagreement is the point.

**Dialogue anchors**

> **Mateus:** You crossed the world to spend lives you cannot name.
>
> **Nikola:** And you learned every name so the butcher would let you choose the order.

> **Ren:** Are you two finished deciding what everyone else believes?

Mateus invokes the chamber seal when Nikola moves to sever the register. He frames the duel as the only way to prevent an uncontrolled ward collapse.

### `a2-06-clean-conscience-duel` — The Duel of Clean Conscience

**Objective:** break three blood-authorized seals and reduce Mateus to a nonlethal yield threshold. Emptying his health bar with a lethal finisher is intercepted by the Lance's council-bound ward and converted into a disarm unless a later-act route explicitly revisits this memory.

The point is not to remove player agency. Act II is the story's formation event; Mateus must survive for the central partnership and later political choice to exist. Lethal authority becomes a real branch only after the player has enough knowledge to understand its consequences.

#### Phase 1 — The Bishop

Mateus fights with measured staff/sword-cane spacing, Umbral bolts, and formal ward geometry.

- He punishes direct repetition.
- One seal is exposed when his long litany completes.
- Nikola's Ward Step and Ren's tag angle are the intended answer.
- Dialogue remains controlled and theological.

#### Phase 2 — The Inquisitor

Mateus uses register chains, marked zones, and delayed bell sentences.

- The player must release a bound name token rather than attack through it.
- Ren can redirect one tracking chain.
- Nikola can sever a seal only after its owner-mark is exposed.
- Mateus's language becomes administrative: households, quotas, acceptable losses.

#### Phase 3 — The Vampire

His composure fails. He uses blood-step movement, self-damaging rites, and a predatory grab he previously claimed his system existed to prevent.

- Crimson Litany has a spectacular but readable commitment and long cooldown.
- Each use harms Mateus and destabilizes the chamber ward.
- The final seal opens only when the player declines a greedy punish long enough to protect the exit lane.
- The finishing prompt is **Yield**, not **Execute**.

#### Boss readability requirements

- animation commitment and post-animation cooldown remain visibly separate;
- Mateus may move after recovering from an animation while his strongest art remains on cooldown;
- every tracking chain shows its owner and break condition;
- phase dialogue never obscures telegraphs;
- the nonlethal threshold is stated in the HUD before the final phase;
- Ren's tag entrance and exit cannot place either character inside a hazard;
- defeat restarts at the boss door with all prior evidence choices preserved.

### `a2-07-a-name-from-europe` — A Name From Europe

Nikola pins Mateus by the sleeve rather than the throat. Mateus studies the grip, recovery stance, Lance seal, and English-influenced fencing detail inherited through Nikola's maternal line.

**Recognition exchange**

> **Mateus:** English weight in the shoulder. Balkan recovery at the wrist. And that spear.
>
> **Nikola:** You know my family?
>
> **Mateus:** Your family was given its blessing so someone would kill men like me.

Mateus identifies:

- the Severed Dragon mark as a covenant designed to sever vampiric authority from political office;
- the Dražanić claim of descent from warriors associated with the Dracul campaign;
- the hidden truth that women, marriage contracts, and adopted names repeatedly carried the covenant when the advertised male line failed;
- a translated Dracul account he once gave Kurozane;
- the possibility that Kurozane used that precedent to turn anti-invasion emergency power into immortal government.

Mateus does not deliver the whole lore archive. He gives enough to prove that the Lance and Kurozane's bell system belong to the same historical echo.

The player chooses what Nikola demands first:

1. **Names of the living prisoners.**
2. **The route by which Kurozane learned the rite.**
3. **Proof that Mateus ever saved anyone.**

All answers become available later. The first demand changes Mateus's judgment of Nikola and the order of the post-boss evidence.

If the Senda clue was found, Nikola may ask who Mateus fears losing. Mateus refuses to name Miyo. The refusal protects her and repeats his habit of deciding what others may know.

### `a2-08-broken-protection` — Broken Protection

Kurozane's purification force rings the public bell from above. The threshold ward begins drawing lesser Oni toward the people it was supposed to protect. This proves that Mateus's guarantee has been revoked or was never fully honored.

Mateus performs the first costly action of his turn:

- he gives Ren the master path through his ward;
- he opens every remaining cell;
- he burns his court license, making himself targetable by the bell network;
- he joins Nikola for a temporary two-man concord that severs the threshold without collapsing the church.

This is the first story use of **Black Sun Concord**:

- Mateus enters the blood-authorized channel;
- Nikola's Radiance bounds it;
- both attacks retain their own damage typing and cooldown;
- neither man controls the other;
- the move breaks the ward object and opens the evacuation route rather than serving as a friendship super-move.

After the fight, the Quiet Cross council chooses Mateus's status. The player may recommend, but not dictate:

| Recommendation | Council implementation | Carry-forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bind him as a source | rotating guards, no unsupervised ministry, route disclosures required | earlier access to court routes; lower immediate public disclosure |
| Make his office public | recorded testimony and visible removal of the license plaque | stronger evidence; higher short-term pursuit |
| Hold judgment locally | Mateus travels only when a council escort authorizes it | slower information flow; strongest local legitimacy |

Mateus becomes a **bound source and conditional combat guest**, not a forgiven friend. The exact permanent active roster is reserved for the full-act plan.

**Closing image:** the public bell hangs silent. Its black approval plaque lies cracked beside the intact church door. Ren hands Mateus a bundle of cell keys instead of his sword.

**Dialogue anchor**

> **Mateus:** Am I your prisoner?
>
> **Ren:** Not mine. That is the first useful thing you should learn.
